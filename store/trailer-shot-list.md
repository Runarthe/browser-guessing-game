# Confetti: Pocket Party — trailer shot list

A 45-second cut. For a party game the trailer does most of the selling, and
almost all of the deciding happens in the first three seconds.

Two rules that matter more than anything below:

1. **Real gameplay only.** No mocked-up UI, no footage of a build that does not
   exist. Buyers are unusually good at spotting it, and Valve's rules expect
   the trailer to represent the game as it actually is.
2. **Shoot people, not just screens.** The reason to own a party game is the
   room it creates. A phone in a hand and a face reacting sells this far
   better than a clean capture of a canvas.

---

## The cut

### 0:00–0:03 — The hook

The single most important shot. Someone's hand holds up a phone showing the
join screen; behind it, slightly out of focus, the TV or monitor shows the
Confetti lobby with the QR code.

No logo yet. No text yet. Just the idea landing: *the controller is the thing
already in your pocket.*

> **Optional caption:** `YOUR PHONE IS THE CONTROLLER`

Cut on the moment their character pops into the lobby.

---

### 0:03–0:08 — The room fills

Fast montage, roughly one second each:

- Second phone scanning the code
- Lobby player list gaining three or four characters in quick succession
- A wide shot of four or five people on a sofa, phones up, already talking

This establishes the pitch — *one copy, everyone plays* — without a word of
explanation.

---

### 0:08–0:13 — The wheel

The minigame wheel spinning and landing. This is your natural act break and
the game already does the work: the spin sound, the announcement popup, the
countdown.

Let the audio carry it. Do not cut away early — the anticipation is the point.

---

### 0:13–0:33 — The minigames

Twenty seconds, roughly two to three seconds each. Lead with the modes that
tested best, and pick the moment in each that reads instantly at small size:

| Mode | The shot to capture |
|---|---|
| **Blast Brawl** | A blast chain going off with two players caught in it |
| **Vanishing Grid** | A player dropping through a floor, camera following down |
| **Polygon Pong** | A fast multi-ball rally, ideally a near-miss save |
| **Place It** | Several pins landing on the world map at once, then the reveal |
| **Balloon** | The pump, the pump, the *pop* — end on the burst |
| **Pocket Racers** | Two cars colliding into a corner |
| **Fire Escape** | The flame wall closing in behind the runners |
| **Curling** | A stone knocking a rival clean off the ice |

Cut on impacts, not on movement. Every cut should land on something happening:
a pop, a collision, a fall, a reveal.

**Sound is doing half the work here.** Cut to the game's own effects rather
than laying music over the top of everything.

---

### 0:33–0:38 — The people

Pull back out to the room. Someone throwing their arms up. Someone groaning.
Two people arguing at a screen.

This is the shot that tells a browser what buying it actually gets them, and
it is the one most indie party trailers forget.

---

### 0:38–0:45 — Title and close

- Champion ceremony from the game, confetti and all — it is already built for
  exactly this moment
- Cut to black
- Logo: **CONFETTI — POCKET PARTY**
- One line beneath: `2–8 players · everyone plays from their phone`
- `WISHLIST NOW` and the Steam logo

---

## Capture notes

- **Record at 1920×1080, 60fps.** The game runs in a window; set it to a clean
  1080p size before capturing so nothing needs upscaling.
- **Turn the music down in-game and add it in the edit.** You want control of
  the mix, and the sound effects are the part that sells the impacts.
- **Capture far more than you need.** Play whole sessions and harvest the good
  moments. Trying to stage a specific play usually looks staged.
- **Get phone footage separately** — a second camera on hands and faces while a
  session runs. It cuts together fine and it is the hardest footage to fake.
- **The first frame is the thumbnail.** Whatever sits at 0:00 is what people
  see before they press play, so make it the phone-and-screen shot, not black.

---

## Screenshots (5+ needed, 1920×1080)

Pick for variety and instant legibility, not for what you are proudest of:

1. The lobby with the QR code and a full player list — states the hook
2. Place It, mid-reveal, pins scattered across the map
3. Vanishing Grid mid-fall, floors visible below
4. Blast Brawl at peak chaos
5. The champion ceremony
6. The unlockables screen — signals depth and reason to keep playing

## Capsule art sizes

Upload at the larger size; Steam displays at half. Confirmed against
[Steamworks documentation](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/assets/standard)
and current guides — worth re-checking before you commission or build them.

| Asset | Upload | Displays at |
|---|---|---|
| Main capsule | 1232×706 | 616×353 |
| Header capsule | 920×430 | 460×215 |
| Small capsule | 462×174 | 231×87 |
| Vertical capsule | 748×896 | 374×448 |
| Library capsule | 600×900 | — |

**The small capsule is the hard one.** Steam auto-generates 184×69 and 120×45
versions from it for search rails, so the game name has to stay readable at
roughly the size of a postage stamp. Design that one first, at its smallest
size, and scale up — not the other way round.

Your icon already solves part of this: the bold **C** on the party gradient
reads at 16px, so it will survive the capsule shrink.
